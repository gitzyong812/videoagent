\begin{abstract}
Long-form video understanding must locate sparse evidence and reason over long-range spatio-temporal interactions under lossy sampling and limited context.
Recent agentic approaches build video captions into searchable indexes for iterative querying, but flat text and embeddings provide limited entity-relational structure and retrieval traceability.
We propose the \textbf{S}cene \textbf{G}raph-\textbf{D}riven Agent (SGDAgent), which converts open-ended caption search into structured candidate verification.
Specifically, we (1)~design a hierarchical spatio-temporal scene graph structure for fine-grained video semantics representation, (2)~build a bottom-up construction pipeline to generate such scene graphs, and (3)~design a scene graph-driven agent following a propose-verify-answer paradigm with graph-based retrieval tools to search video content in a structured way.
Across four benchmarks, SGDAgent remains competitive with baselines using stronger reasoning models.
Under matched LVBench settings, adding the scene-graph hierarchy to the caption index raises end-to-end accuracy from 54.8\% to 64.4\%, and the three control strategies further raise it to 73.0\%.
Agent trajectory analysis further shows that the gains arise from converting open retrieval into graph-anchored candidate verification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% - Para 1: Long-video understanding challenges -

% - Para 2: Current approaches and their limitations -

Long-form video understanding requires semantic analysis and reasoning over hour-scale videos and poses three fundamental challenges: \emph{incomplete coverage} from lossy frame sampling, \emph{imprecise reasoning} over long-range spatio-temporal entity interactions that flat embeddings or text fail to capture, and \emph{imprecise localization} of brief or cross-segment evidence buried in background noise.
Recent agentic video reasoning approaches~\cite{deepvideo2025,videoagent,drvideo2025} convert videos into caption-centric indexes and let LLMs iteratively query them.
Yet \emph{the evidence space remains fundamentally unstructured}: flat text and visual embeddings cannot represent high-order spatio-temporal semantics or support traceable retrieval.
Advances in multi-round retrieval~\cite{videoagent}, grounding-verification pipelines~\cite{videomind}, and multi-granularity toolkits~\cite{videoseek} improve \emph{how the agent searches} without restructuring \emph{what it searches over}.

% - Para 3: Scene graphs as structured representation -

Scene graphs encode visual entities and their spatio-temporal interactions as graph nodes and edges~\cite{scenegraph1,tpt2023}.
They provide the entity-level and relational grounding that flat representations lack.
Their structured form supports compact semantic modeling, explicit relational constraints, and precise retrieval through graph search~\cite{scenegraph1,scenegraph2,qiu2025step}.
Prior work has explored hierarchical interlacement graphs for video scene graph generation~\cite{hig2024}, topological semantic graphs for long-video reasoning~\cite{videomindpalace2025}, and entity-relation graphs for agentic frame selection~\cite{graphvideoagent2025,egagent2026}.
Other methods convert video frames into textual descriptions or simpler structured representations and build large-model agents for visual reasoning~\cite{fei2024videoofthought,videoagent,omagent2024,vagent2025,deepvideo2025,drvideo2025}.
However, two gaps prevent their direct application to long-form video QA\@.
First, existing methods focus on frame-level or segment-level local relations, lacking unified cross-scale hierarchical organization to capture both instantaneous details and cross-segment causal chains~\cite{tpt2023,vagent2025}.
Second, existing graph-aware systems either target graph generation, feed a graph directly to an LLM, or use a dynamic entity graph for frame selection, whereas SGDAgent uses a three-level scene graph as the primary retrieval index in a propose-verify-answer loop~\cite{hig2024,videomindpalace2025,graphvideoagent2025,egagent2026,vagent2025}.

% - Para 4: Our method -- representation + agent workflow -

In this work, we present the \textbf{S}cene \textbf{G}raph-\textbf{D}riven Agent (SGDAgent), a novel framework that transforms open-ended caption-based search into structured, graph-guided candidate verification.
Firstly, we devise a hierarchical spatio-temporal scene graph architecture that encodes fine-grained video semantics across multiple temporal scales. Then, we develop a bottom-up offline pipeline that constructs and indexes frame-level graphs, segment-level graphs, and a global event graph through a shared entity registry. Finally, we introduce a reasoning agent that uses graph retrieval to propose candidate regions and targeted frame inspection to verify them before answering.
SGDAgent therefore separates structured localization from visual confirmation within an interpretable propose-verify-answer process.

% - Para 5: Results -

These designs yield substantial gains under controlled settings.
On LVBench~\cite{lvbench}, augmenting the caption index with the scene-graph hierarchy raises end-to-end accuracy from 54.8\% to 64.4\% and answered accuracy from 65.7\% to 72.2\%.
Adding the routing, confidence-gating, and terminal-convergence strategies further raises end-to-end accuracy to 73.0\%, increases the answer rate from 83.5\% to 98.4\%, and reduces the failed-to-terminate (FTT) rate from 16.5\% to 1.6\%.
Across four benchmarks, SGDAgent with the lighter \texttt{o4-mini} reasoning model approaches DVD~\cite{deepvideo2025} with the stronger \texttt{o3} reasoning model on LVBench and LongVideoBench~\cite{longvideobench2024}, although these cross-method results are reference comparisons rather than fully controlled experiments.
Ablation and trajectory analyses show that frame-level and segment-level graphs progressively improve candidate discovery, the global event graph primarily reduces search cost, and the control strategies improve answer quality and convergence.

% - Para 6: Contributions -

In summary, we contribute (1)~a \textbf{hierarchical spatio-temporal scene graph representation} comprising frame-level graphs, segment-level graphs, and a global event graph connected through a shared entity registry and constructed by a bottom-up offline pipeline; (2)~a \textbf{scene graph-driven agent} that combines graph-guided candidate proposal, visual verification, caption backoff, and uncertainty-aware control; and (3)~\textbf{comprehensive evaluation} on four benchmarks, including matched-setting ablations that separate the effects of video-index composition, scene-graph hierarchy, and agent control.



\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Long-video understanding.}
Vision foundation models and multimodal large language models have advanced short-clip understanding~\cite{radford2021CLIP,li2022GLIP,llava2023,video-llava2024,bai2025qwen3vl,caba2015activitynet}, but hour-scale videos still present three persistent challenges: large temporal spans, complex spatio-temporal interactions, and sparse critical evidence.
Long videos require discontinuous sampling~\cite{deepvideo2025} or token compression~\cite{wang2025adaretake}, which can lose detail~\cite{lvbench,longvideobench2024}, while text descriptions cannot explicitly capture long-range entity dependencies~\cite{zhang2024simplellm} and agents must progressively locate fleeting evidence~\cite{videoagent,omagent2024}.
Existing methods either extend context through longer windows, token compression, or hierarchical representations~\cite{gemini15,qwen,internvl,seed,radford2021CLIP,li2022GLIP,llava2023,video-llava2024,bai2025qwen3vl,longvu2025,shu2025video,apollo2025}, or retrieve evidence through selective sampling and agentic search~\cite{deepvideo2025,mrvideo,videoseek,zhang2024simplellm,videorag2025,videotree2025,drvideo2025,yang2025vca}.
These approaches reduce input scale or search cost, but sparse evidence must still be located within compressed or selectively sampled representations.
SGDAgent instead augments captions with a scene graph index that grounds candidate proposal in entities, relations, and temporal constraints.

\textbf{Scene graph representation.}
Scene graphs encode visual entities and their spatio-temporal interactions as graph nodes and edges~\cite{xu2017scene,tpt2023}.
Traditional detect-then-classify pipelines~\cite{xu2017scene,motifs2018} have evolved toward open-vocabulary generation with vision-language models~\cite{frompixel2024,vs32023,hyperglm2025}, although cross-segment association and temporal dynamics remain challenging.
HIG~\cite{hig2024} models hierarchical interlacement for video scene graph generation, while VideoMindPalace~\cite{videomindpalace2025} organizes long-video observations into an environment-grounded topological graph that is supplied to an LLM for reasoning.
GraphVideoAgent~\cite{graphvideoagent2025} maintains a dynamic entity-relation graph to guide frame selection, and EGAgent~\cite{egagent2026} combines temporally annotated entity scene graphs with structured visual and audio search over very long egocentric videos.
Together, these studies establish graph structure as useful for generation, memory organization, and evidence search in long videos.
SGDAgent differs by using frame-level graphs, segment-level graphs, and a global event graph as the retrieval index of a propose-verify-answer agent, where graph retrieval localizes candidates and targeted frame inspection confirms the facts.

\textbf{Agentic video reasoning.}
Directly feeding frame or clip tokens to language models~\cite{video-llava2024,videochat2025} is constrained by context length and computation on hour-scale videos.
Text-centered methods address this limitation through joint clip descriptions in LLoVi~\cite{zhang2024simplellm}, retrieval-augmented indexing in VideoRAG~\cite{videorag2025}, query-adaptive hierarchies in VideoTree~\cite{videotree2025}, or progressively refined document memory in DrVideo~\cite{drvideo2025}.
Interactive approaches include multi-round frame retrieval in VideoAgent~\cite{videoagent}, ReAct-based search over a multi-granularity database in DVD~\cite{deepvideo2025,react2022}, and human-like seeking tools in VideoSeek~\cite{videoseek}.
VideoMind~\cite{videomind} separates localization, verification, and answering, VCA~\cite{yang2025vca} uses curiosity-driven tree exploration, and Vgent~\cite{vagent2025} performs graph-structured retrieval and reasoning.
Other directions include multi-agent systems~\cite{omagent2024}, neuro-symbolic reasoning~\cite{neuro2020,cai2025naver}, and end-to-end post-training on agent trajectories~\cite{team2026kimi,zeng2026glm}.
These agents improve navigation flexibility, but vary in whether retrieved evidence is explicitly structured and independently verified.
SGDAgent complements these approaches with retrieval over a scene-graph index to propose entity- and temporally grounded candidates for subsequent visual verification.


\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}


SGDAgent converts open-ended caption search into graph-guided candidate localization followed by visual verification.
We first define and construct its hierarchical spatio-temporal scene graph index, then present the propose-verify-answer agent.

\subsection{Hierarchical Scene Graph Representation}
\label{sec:representation}

SGDAgent represents a video $V$ with a caption index and a three-level spatio-temporal scene graph hierarchy (Figure~\ref{fig:graph}(c)):
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{I}(V) = \bigl\{C(V),\; G^{f},\; G^{s},\; G^{g}\bigr\},
\end{equation}
$C(V)$ is the caption index, while $G^{f}=\{G_i^{f}\}_{i=1}^{N}$, $G^{s}=\{G_j^{s}\}_{j=1}^{M}$, and $G^{g}=(V^{g},\,\mathcal{E}^{g})$ denote the frame-level, segment-level, and global event graphs, respectively.
All graph levels share an entity registry $E^{\mathrm{video}}$ that stores each entity's ID, type, attributes, description, and temporal span for cross-scale identity alignment.

\textbf{Frame-level graph.}
Each frame-level graph captures an entity-relation snapshot at a single keyframe:
\begin{equation}
  G_i^{f} = (E_i^{f},\; R_i^{f},\; t_i,\; c_i),
\end{equation}
$E_i^{f} \subseteq E^{\mathrm{video}}$ contains visible entities, $R_i^{f}$ contains triples $(s,p,o)$ with $s,o \in E_i^{f}$, $t_i$ is the timestamp, and $c_i$ is the aligned caption.

\textbf{Segment-level graph.}
Each segment-level graph aggregates entity interactions over a short temporal window:
\begin{equation}
  G_j^{s} = (E_j^{s},\; R_j^{s},\;
  [t_j^{\mathrm{s}}, t_j^{\mathrm{e}}],\; F_j,\; s_j),
\end{equation}
$E_j^{s}$ contains active entities, $R_j^{s}$ contains temporal relations, $[t_j^{\mathrm{s}},t_j^{\mathrm{e}}]$ is the segment interval, $F_j$ contains supporting frame IDs, and $s_j$ is the summary.
Each relation $(s,p,o,t_{\mathrm{start}},t_{\mathrm{end}},\mathcal{F})$ is grounded by its temporal extent and supporting frames.

\textbf{Global event graph.}
The global event graph $G^{g}=(V^{g},\,\mathcal{E}^{g})$ organizes higher-level event semantics over segments.
Each event node $n_k \in V^{g}$ is
\begin{equation}
  n_k = (P_k,\; R_k,\;
  [t_k^{\mathrm{s}}, t_k^{\mathrm{e}}],\; S_k,\; F_k,\; \sigma_k),
\end{equation}
$P_k$ and $R_k$ contain participants and salient relations, $[t_k^{\mathrm{s}},t_k^{\mathrm{e}}]$ is the event interval, $S_k$ and $F_k$ provide segment and frame provenance, and $\sigma_k$ is the summary.
Nodes are typed as \emph{unit events} or \emph{group events}, while edges encode \texttt{before}, \texttt{overlaps}, \texttt{entity-\allowbreak carryover}, \texttt{same-\allowbreak thread}, and \texttt{parent-\allowbreak child} relations.

\subsection{Bottom-up Scene Graph Construction}
\label{sec:construction}

The bottom-up pipeline extracts frame-level graphs, merges them into segment-level graphs, and organizes the segments into a global event graph (Figure~\ref{fig:graph}(b)).

\textbf{Frame-level graph extraction and entity registration.}
A VLM extracts typed entities, attributes, and relations from uniformly sampled keyframes.
Each entity is greedily aligned to $E^{\mathrm{video}}$ using name, description, and type similarity.
Matched entities are merged, and the rest are registered as new.
If extraction is empty, neighboring captions provide coarse entities to prevent graph gaps.

\textbf{Segment-level graph merging.}
Frame-level graphs are merged within overlapping temporal windows.
Matching triples form temporally grounded relations while preserving provenance and cross-window links.

\textbf{Global event graph construction.}
Temporally adjacent and semantically overlapping segments are clustered into events.
Long or discontinuous clusters are split into unit events and organized into group events.
Temporal, entity-continuity, thread, and hierarchy links form the global event graph.

\textbf{Quality control.}
Lightweight filtering and schema validation suppress noisy entities and relations, while global event nodes retain confidence and uncertainty estimates for downstream gating.


\subsection{Scene Graph-Driven Agent}
\label{sec:agent}

\subsubsection{Adaptive reasoning agent loop.}
Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} summarizes the agent's observe-reason-act loop~\cite{react2022} over the prebuilt video index.
At step~$t$, state~$H$ contains the question, retrieved candidates, accumulated evidence, and controller signals.
The LLM proposes an action, query-type routing maps it to $a_t \in \mathcal{T}$, and the selected tool updates~$H$.
A final answer is accepted only after confidence gating, and any graph-derived claim requires qualified visual evidence from $FI$.
If the step budget~$B$ is exhausted, terminal readout returns the best-supported option from the trajectory.

\subsubsection{Tool chain.}
The agent acts over the tool chain $\mathcal{T}=\{GR,\,\allowbreak SR,\,\allowbreak FR,\,\allowbreak FI,\,\allowbreak CB,\,\allowbreak \mathrm{Answer}\}$.
$GR$, $SR$, and $FR$ query the global event, segment-level, and frame-level graphs to propose candidates at progressively finer granularity.
The frame-inspection tool $FI$ verifies candidates against raw frames, while caption backoff $CB$ provides a complementary text channel.
Each graph query returns ranked regions, provenance pointers, and controller signals for confidence-aware descent and verification.

The tool interfaces are
\[
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{$
  \begin{array}{rcl}
  \mathrm{GraphRetrieve}_{\ell}: & (q,\ \mathrm{scope},\ k) & \mapsto\ (\mathrm{regions}_{1:k},\ \mathrm{signals}), \\
  \mathrm{FrameInspect}: & (\mathrm{window},\ q) & \mapsto\ \mathrm{evidence}, \\
  \mathrm{CaptionBackoff}: & (q) & \mapsto\ \mathrm{summary}.
  \end{array}
$}
\]
All three graph retrievers share a common scorer that ranks a candidate region $w$ against the query $q$ by
\begin{equation}
  S(w \mid q) \;=\; S_{\mathrm{lex}}(w,q) \;+\;
  S_{\mathrm{soft}}(w,q) \;+\; S_{\mathrm{temp}}(w,q),
\end{equation}
$S_{\mathrm{lex}}$ scores token overlap over entity names, relation predicates, and region summaries.
$S_{\mathrm{soft}}$ uses embedding cosine similarity when available and lexical fuzzy matching otherwise.
$S_{\mathrm{temp}}$ matches explicit time spans and relative temporal cues in $q$.
The $\mathrm{scope}$ argument forwards a coarse time constraint, while provenance pointers link each region to supporting nodes at the next finer level.

\emph{Global event graph retrieval (GR).}
$GR$ searches the full global event graph for causal, plot-level, or video-spanning questions and returns ranked event windows with inter-event relations and segment provenance.
The selected event span scopes the subsequent $SR$ call to a smaller temporal range.
For temporal or causal sequences, event edges also determine the traversal order.

\emph{Segment-level graph retrieval (SR).}
$SR$ searches segment-level graphs globally or within a $GR$ scope and returns ranked windows, temporal triples, keyframe provenance, and controller signals.
Its windows feed $FI$ directly or constrain $FR$ when object- or relation-level grounding is needed.
The signals flag fuzzy matches, overly broad windows, and visual details that the graph cannot resolve.

\emph{Frame-level graph retrieval (FR).}
$FR$ retrieves object-level details such as attributes, identity, counts, and spatial relations within a narrow $SR$ scope.
It returns ranked keyframes with timestamped entity--attribute--relation snapshots.
These timestamps bound $FI$ to short windows, while the graph content remains candidate evidence until visually confirmed.

\emph{Frame inspection (FI) and caption backoff (CB).}
$FI$ implements visual verification by inspecting raw pixels within a graph-proposed window and returns question-conditioned evidence for the final answer.
$CB$ queries the embedding-based caption index when graph retrieval lacks the question's surface form.
Caption backoff aids candidate discovery but cannot validate graph-derived claims.

\begin{algorithm}[!tb]
  \caption{SGDAgent reasoning loop with control strategies: query-type routing (R), confidence gating (CG), and terminal convergence (T).}
  \label{alg:search}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \footnotesize
  \REQUIRE Video index $\mathcal{I}(V)=\{C(V),G^f,G^s,G^g\}$, query $q$, step budget $B$
  \ENSURE Answer $\hat{a}$
  \STATE $H \leftarrow \mathrm{InitState}(q)$
  \FOR{$t=1,\ldots,B$}
      \STATE $a_t \leftarrow \mathrm{QueryTypeRoute}(\mathrm{LLM}(H),q)$
        \hfill \textcolor{blue!60!black}{\scriptsize\textbf{[R]}}
      \IF{$a_t \in \{GR,SR,FR\}$}
          \STATE $H \leftarrow \mathrm{GraphRetrieve}(H,a_t,q,\mathcal{I}(V))$
          \IF{$\mathrm{WeakGraphSignal}(H)$}
              \STATE $H \leftarrow \mathrm{CaptionBackoff}(H,q,C(V))$
                \hfill \textcolor{blue!60!black}{\scriptsize\textbf{[CG]}}
          \ENDIF
      \ELSIF{$a_t=CB$}
          \STATE $H \leftarrow \mathrm{CaptionBackoff}(H,q,C(V))$
      \ELSIF{$a_t=FI$}
          \STATE $H \leftarrow \mathrm{FrameInspect}(H,q)$
      \ELSIF{$a_t=\mathrm{Answer} \land \mathrm{ConfidenceGate}(H)$}
          \RETURN $\mathrm{Readout}(H)$
            \hfill \textcolor{blue!60!black}{\scriptsize\textbf{[CG]}}
      \ENDIF
  \ENDFOR
  \RETURN $\mathrm{TerminalReadout}(H)$
    \hfill \textcolor{blue!60!black}{\scriptsize\textbf{[T]}}
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsubsection{Agent control strategies.}
As shown in Algorithm~\ref{alg:search}, controller signals encode match quality, region granularity, the need for frame inspection, and the recommended next action.
\textbf{Strategy~R} (query-type routing) sends questions about counts, colors, and on-screen text directly to $FI$, routes causal and plot-level questions through $GR$, and starts localization questions at $SR$.
\textbf{Strategy~CG} (confidence gating) prevents answering under \emph{localize-only} or \emph{weak-match} signals, sends coarse candidates to narrow-window frame inspection, and redirects weak graph results to $CB$ when appropriate.
The finish gate accepts graph-derived claims only when supported by qualified $FI$ evidence.
\textbf{Strategy~T} (terminal convergence) returns the best-supported option from the accumulated trace when the step budget $B$ is exhausted, reducing FTT outcomes.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

% ============================================================
%  Section 4.1  --  Experimental Setup
% ============================================================

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Datasets.}
We evaluate SGDAgent on LVBench full~\cite{lvbench}, LongVideoBench validation~\cite{longvideobench2024}, Video-MME Long without subtitles~\cite{videomme2024}, and EgoSchema Val 500~\cite{egoschema2023}.
All SGDAgent results in Table~\ref{tab:main_comparison} are our runs on these splits.
LVBench, which contains 1{,}549 questions over 103 long videos, is additionally used for matched-setting ablations and trajectory analysis.

% ============================================================
%  Main Comparison Table -- spans 4 benchmarks
% ============================================================


\textbf{Implementation details.}
We use \texttt{o4-mini} as the reasoning model and \texttt{gpt-4.1} for frame-level graph extraction.
Entity alignment weights name, description, and type similarity by 0.7, 0.2, and 0.1, respectively, with a merge threshold of 0.78.
Segment-level graphs are constructed using 30-second sliding windows with 5-second overlap.
Global event clustering uses a maximum temporal gap of 12~seconds, relation- and predicate-overlap thresholds of 0.18 and 0.22, and shared entities as an additional merge cue.
Visual verification uses \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini} with at most 50~frames per call, while $GR$, $SR$, and $FR$ return up to 5, 6, and 8 candidates, respectively.
Building the LVBench scene graph index takes about 7.94 hours with four workers, including about 121 seconds for global event graph construction.
The video index is constructed once and reused across all questions.
As for caption index, we follow DVD~\cite{deepvideo2025} construction and embedding-based retrieval protocol for fair comparison.
Matched LVBench configurations share the reasoning model, frame-inspection VLM, captions, answer choices, and budgets, varying only index composition, scene-graph hierarchy, and agent control.

\textbf{Evaluation metrics.}
End-to-end accuracy is correct answers divided by all questions, answered accuracy is correct answers divided by answered questions, and answer rate is answered questions divided by all questions.
Answer rate equals $1-\mathrm{FTT}$, where FTT includes empty outputs, runtime failures, and non-convergent trajectories.
For our runs, all percentages are computed independently from raw counts and rounded to one decimal place.
As in caption-index agents~\cite{videoagent,deepvideo2025,drvideo2025}, we evaluate the scene graph as an intermediate retrieval index through downstream QA and ablations.
Open-vocabulary video scene-graph generation lacks a standard benchmark covering cross-frame identity and hierarchical events~\cite{frompixel2024,hig2024,hyperglm2025}.
We therefore inspect a small subset and find that salient entities, relations, and temporal links are generally preserved, but this qualitative check does not replace intrinsic evaluation.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_comparison} reports our SGDAgent runs on four benchmarks alongside published results.
Cross-method comparisons are for reference because settings differ, while component attribution relies on the matched LVBench ablations.
With \texttt{o4-mini}, SGDAgent achieves 73.0\% on LVBench and 71.2\% on LongVideoBench.
These results approach the reported DVD~\cite{deepvideo2025} scores of 74.2\% and 71.6\%, respectively, obtained with the stronger \texttt{o3} reasoning model.
Our \texttt{o3} runs achieve 75.9\% on LVBench, 74.0\% on LongVideoBench, 69.0\% on Video-MME Long~\cite{videomme2024}, and 77.4\% on EgoSchema~\cite{egoschema2023}.
The corresponding DVD results are 74.2\%, 71.6\%, 67.3\%, and 76.6\%.
These reference positions are consistent with the matched-setting finding that structured candidate proposal can complement the reasoning model, but they are not used to isolate the source of the gain.

\subsection{Ablation Study}

Table~\ref{tab:component_ablation} isolates the effects of video-index composition, scene-graph hierarchy, and agent control under matched LVBench settings.
All configurations share the same reasoning model, candidate answers, and budget.
We denote the caption-only baseline by \emph{Base} and the complete-index configuration without agent control by \mbox{\emph{SGDAgent}\textsuperscript{0}}.
\emph{Base} superscripts list index components, \emph{SGDAgent} superscripts list control strategies, and the plain name denotes the full system.


\textbf{Effect of video-index composition and scene-graph hierarchy.}
The graph-only configuration \mbox{\emph{Base}\textsuperscript{F+S+G}} reaches 58.0\% end-to-end accuracy, 3.2 points above the caption-only \emph{Base}.
Adding the frame-level graph to \emph{Base} yields \mbox{\emph{Base}\textsuperscript{C+F}} and raises end-to-end accuracy from 54.8\% to 59.2\%, while further adding the segment-level graph yields \mbox{\emph{Base}\textsuperscript{C+F+S}} and raises it to 62.8\%.
Adding the global event graph yields the complete-index configuration \mbox{\emph{SGDAgent}\textsuperscript{0}}, raising end-to-end accuracy to 64.4\% and answered accuracy to 72.2\% while reducing LLM calls from 9.6 to 2.9 and frame-inspection calls from 5.1 to 1.4.
These results reveal complementary roles: captions provide broad lexical coverage, frame-level and segment-level graphs support structured candidate discovery, and the global event graph primarily improves routing efficiency before visual verification.


\textbf{Effect of agent control strategies.}
Adding query-type routing (R) to the no-control configuration \mbox{\emph{SGDAgent}\textsuperscript{0}} yields \mbox{\emph{SGDAgent}\textsuperscript{R}}, raising answered accuracy from 72.2\% to 75.1\% and the answer rate from 89.2\% to 92.6\%.
Adding confidence gating (CG) to the routing configuration \mbox{\emph{SGDAgent}\textsuperscript{R}} yields \mbox{\emph{SGDAgent}\textsuperscript{R+CG}}, raising end-to-end accuracy from 69.5\% to 70.7\% and the answer rate from 92.6\% to 94.8\%, with answered accuracy changing slightly from 75.1\% to 74.6\%.
Adding terminal convergence (T) to the routing configuration \mbox{\emph{SGDAgent}\textsuperscript{R}} yields \mbox{\emph{SGDAgent}\textsuperscript{R+T}}, raising the answer rate from 92.6\% to 95.6\% and reducing FTT from 7.4\% to 4.4\%, although answered accuracy decreases from 75.1\% to 74.1\%.
These comparisons separate their roles: R contributes most to answered accuracy, whereas CG and T primarily recover otherwise unresolved trajectories through evidence escalation and terminal readout.
Enabling all three strategies yields the full \emph{SGDAgent} configuration, which achieves the best end-to-end accuracy of 73.0\% and answer rate of 98.4\%, with 74.2\% answered accuracy and 1.6\% FTT.

% -----------
%  Experiment 3: Efficiency
% -----------

\textbf{Efficiency analysis.}
Frame-level and segment-level retrieval trade computation for deeper candidate discovery, increasing LLM calls from 3.7 to 9.6 and frame inspections from 1.1 to 5.1.
The global event graph compresses this search to 2.9 LLM calls and 1.4 frame inspections by routing local retrieval to a smaller temporal scope.
Control adds modest deliberation (2.9$\to$3.4 LLM calls) but reduces frame inspections (1.4$\to$1.0).
The full system still uses fewer calls than the caption-only \emph{Base} (3.7 and 1.1) while reaching 73.0\% end-to-end accuracy.
The complete design therefore shifts computation from repeated local exploration to global proposal and selective visual verification.

%  Section 4.4  --  Analysis on Agentic Reasoning Behavior
% ============================================================

\subsection{Analysis on Agentic Reasoning Behavior}
\label{sec:analysis}

The ablation identifies the contribution of each component, while Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory} illustrates how graph-guided search changes a reasoning trajectory.
Graph retrieval replaces broad caption exploration with coarse-to-fine candidate localization followed by focused visual verification.

\textbf{Case study: escaping the frame-inspect trap.}\quad
The question asks: ``Why does the man follow the woman in red after they meet for the third time?'' (correct answer: the woman has his book).
The question is challenging because the agent must first locate a specific \emph{repeated event} within an hour-long video and then infer the \emph{motivation} behind the subsequent action from an object-possession relation.
The caption-only \emph{Base} queries the caption index through $CB$ and performs five subsequent frame inspections, $\mathrm{CB} \!\to\! \mathrm{FI}^{\times 5} \!\to\! \mathrm{Answer}$, but selects the wrong option.
Without structured links among the participants, repeated encounter, and carried object, its broad inspections return fragmented evidence.
This repeated-inspection failure is the \emph{frame-inspect trap}~\cite{deepvideo2025}.

SGDAgent instead follows $\mathrm{GR} \!\to\! \mathrm{SR} \!\to\! \mathrm{FR} \!\to\! \mathrm{FI} \!\to\! \mathrm{Answer}$.
$GR$ proposes event \texttt{evt\_0055} [23:45--24:40] for ``the third encounter,'' reducing the full video to a 55-second candidate window.
$SR$ narrows this window to segment \texttt{seg\_0058} [23:45--24:15] and surfaces the relevant participants and interactions.
$FR$ then retrieves candidate \emph{person--holding--book} triples from keyframes \texttt{fra\_0064} and \texttt{fra\_0086}.
A single $FI$ call on [24:00--24:15] verifies the book-possession relation in raw frames and supports the correct answer.

The graph therefore supplies structured candidates rather than terminal facts: $GR$ and $SR$ localize the event, $FR$ proposes the object relation, and $FI$ confirms it visually.
This trajectory supports the ablation finding that the hierarchy improves reasoning by restructuring search, not by replacing FI-based visual verification.

\section{Conclusion}

SGDAgent combines captions with hierarchical spatio-temporal scene graphs to localize candidates and verifies them through targeted frame inspection.
Across four benchmarks, it remains competitive with agents using stronger reasoning models.
On matched LVBench settings~\cite{lvbench}, the graph index raises end-to-end accuracy from 54.8\% to 64.4\%, and agent control further raises it to 73.0\%.
Ablations show that frame-level and segment-level graphs improve candidate discovery, while the global event graph primarily reduces search cost.

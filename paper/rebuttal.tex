and thank the reviewer for the close reading. We have made a line-by-line revision: the
dangling \texttt{Figure ??} now resolves to Fig.~1 (Sec.~3.2), the flagged typos are fixed

\pt{R1-2: Distinction from Vgent.} The difference is paradigmatic, along two axes.
\emph{Representation:} Vgent builds a single \emph{flat} graph over local frame/segment
relations; we use an LLM to build a \emph{three-level hierarchical} scene graph
(frame$\to$segment$\to$event) unified by a shared entity registry, exposing cross-scale
structure a flat graph cannot. \emph{Reasoning:} Vgent is a fixed retrieve-then-read RAG
pipeline that answers in a single pass; our agent treats the hierarchical graph as its
primary retrieval index and reasons \emph{agentically} over multiple rounds, issuing
multi-level graph queries, verifying each proposal against raw frames, and deciding its
next step until it converges. Together these
drive the +18.2-pp matched-setting gain on LVBench (54.8\%$\to$73.0\%).

\pt{R1-3: The role of the event graph.} We respectfully find the 64.6\%$\to$64.4\%
reading too narrow: it isolates the Stage-3 \emph{increment} and asks it to carry the
contribution alone. \emph{(1)} Our representation is a three-level stack (Base (frame-level)\,+\,Stage~2 (segment-level)\,+\,Stage~3 (event/video-level)),
so the fair comparison is the full stack against Base, where the hierarchy improves
\emph{both} axes: accuracy rises 54.8\%$\to$73.0\% (+18.2\,pp, matched) and the
event layer cuts LLM and frame-inspection calls by 71.5\% and 74.7\% and shortens the
agent's reasoning rounds.
\emph{(2)} In our \emph{follow-up experiment} that strengthens Stage~3, the event layer also lifts accuracy while keeping the efficiency: it reaches \emph{68.5\%} e2e on LVBench, +3.9\,pp over the
segment-only version (64.6\%) and +13.7\,pp over the caption-only Base (54.8\%). We will report these strengthened, matched results in the revision.

\rv{Reviewer D8hx (\#2) -- R2-1: Offline construction cost.} For all 103 LVBench
videos (115.5h), construction costs \$112.74 (12,360 \texttt{gpt-4.1} frame-graph
calls at one keyframe per 5s, $\le$120/video, 7.94h on four workers, and the symbolic
Stage~3 event build adds only 121s), i.e.\ \$0.98 per video-hour. We reuse DVD's open-sourced captions rather than
generating our own, so this is our only added offline cost. End-to-end VQA over all 1549 questions costs \$279.54
(\texttt{o4-mini}) and \$499.31 (\texttt{o3}), so this one-time construction is
only 40\% and 23\% of the recurring online budget it serves.

\pt{R2-2: Cross-benchmark control.} Thanks for pointing this out. On
LVBench the DVD comparison is fully controlled: the same \texttt{o3}
backbone, DVD's own open-sourced captions, and the same video database. On the
other three, DVD released no full reproduction setup, so we cannot match its
pipeline. We still use its captions and report these as best-effort positions.
Other commercial and open models have closed weights or fixed pipelines, so they are reference
positions only. Our claims therefore rest on the fully controlled LVBench comparison and its matched-setting
ablations.

\pt{R2-3: Direct graph quality.} We agree direct graph-quality evaluation is
valuable, but there is not yet a widely accepted hour-scale benchmark with
open-vocabulary entities, relations, temporal alignment, and long-video
annotations. We therefore follow prior long-video agent work such as DVD:
caption/graph indices are evaluated by their end-to-end QA effect rather than
by an intrinsic ``agent-index quality'' score. To make this more concrete, we
also manually checked a small sample of generated graphs; it shows 76.8\%
entity-mention absorption and only a 5.0\% invalid-relation rate. In the
revision, we will report this human-verified sample together with the
caption-free graph-only result, where the answer must come from the graph.

\pt{R2-4: Implementation details.} To give the values asked for, entity alignment
scores name, description, and type similarity with weights 0.7, 0.2, and 0.1, and
merges above a threshold of 0.78. The entity blacklist removes artefacts and
over-generic terms, and segments merge into events at a 12s temporal gap with
relation-overlap thresholds. The composite retrieval score and all remaining thresholds are fully specified in
the appendix, which gives the alignment, gating, and event-construction
algorithms. We will release a fully reproducible repository.

\pt{R2-5: Presentation.} Thanks. Please see R1-1.

\pt{R2-6: Isolating localization from FTT.} Our reported answered accuracy
(ans\_acc) separates the two: the caption-only baseline scores 65.7\% and
SGDAgent 74.2\% (+8.5\,pp), independent of the answer rate, while the FTT drop
(16.5\%$\to$1.6\%) is itself a consequence of structured retrieval converging
where open caption search loops for lack of locatable evidence.

\pt{R3-4: Error propagation \& robustness.} Two mechanisms limit it. First,
the propose-and-verify step gates every graph hit to raw-frame re-inspection before answering
(R2-3), so a hallucinated or mis-aligned node is caught at verification rather
than read out as an answer, reducing upward propagation. Second, the caption-free
graph-only system still reaches 68.5\% (Please see R1-3), evidence that the pipeline
tolerates imperfect frame-level extraction. To quantify this we will add a
robustness analysis that perturbs frame-graph quality and reports the accuracy
sensitivity, and we will discuss [4].

\rv{Reviewer wGgF (\#5) -- R5-1: HIG \& hierarchy novelty.} Thank you. We agree HIG
is relevant prior work and will cite and contrast it. We do not claim hierarchy
itself as novel. The hierarchy differs in both form and purpose: HIG aggregates
interactivities for video scene-graph \emph{generation}, whereas SGDAgent builds a
frame--segment--event graph index as the representation layer for an adaptive
long-video understanding agent. Thus, HIG predicts scene graphs, while we use scene
graphs to guide proposal and verification for long-video QA (Please see R1-2).

\pt{R5-2: Implementation details.} The alignment, retrieval, gating, controller-signal, and
event-construction rules are all specified operationally in the appendix as
pseudocode. Please see R2-4 for the headline values.

\pt{R5-3: Graph-depth ablation.} Please see R1-3. The segment graph drives
fixed-backbone accuracy, and the event graph reduces search burden and, under a
stronger construction, also contributes accuracy (caption-free 68.5\%).

\pt{R5-4: Efficiency evidence.} Beyond online call counts we now also report
dollar cost and construction time (Please see R2-1).
